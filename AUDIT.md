Rail Baron correctness audit — `codex_v1`, starting from `1bb960a`.

The engine had rule defects that can change winners and money transfers despite the original 72 passing tests. The fixes below are uncommitted. No files in `Map Data/` were changed. Physical-game findings are proposals or suspicions, not verified corrections.

Findings, ordered by impact

1. **Fixed and tested — declaring at home during a bonus trip could award a win with less than $200,000 after fees.** [game.py](railbaron/game.py:176), `_start_trip`. A player could arrive normally, reach exactly $200,000, declare at home before the bonus, and become the winner before the $1,000 fee reduced cash to $199,000. The ordinary home-arrival rule requires the winning cash after fees. The win is now deferred when this turn has unpaid fees; the recording contains a pending `declare` event. An initial at-home declaration with no unpaid fees still wins immediately. Regression: `test_declaring_at_home_on_bonus_waits_for_outstanding_fees`. The overlapping immediate-win and bonus clauses need Jake's interpretation; see below.

2. **Fixed and tested — track reuse was permitted outside its narrow exception.** [policy.py](railbaron/policy.py:88), `choose_route`; [game.py](railbaron/game.py:222), `_validate_route`, `_reuse_route`, and `_undeclare`. The rulebook permits reuse only after an interrupted declaration, only of sections ridden on the way home, and only as absolutely necessary. Previously, any failed blocked-route search fell back to an unrestricted shortest path, including during ordinary trips. The engine now records eligible homeward track, rejects reuse elsewhere, and minimizes the number of reused sections before distance. A reused section loses its eligibility for another reuse. Custom routes are checked for track existence, repeated sections, premature passage through the destination, and unnecessary reuse. Regressions cover an ordinary blocked trip, a longer alternate route needing one reuse rather than a shorter route needing two, and invalid custom routes.

3. **Fixed and tested — compressed route searches could jump across used track.** [tables.py](railbaron/tables.py:174), `search`. The once-per-trip rule applies to every section, not just the first and last sections of a compressed stretch. The previous test checked only stretch endpoints and skipped checks when only internal dots were touched. It now checks every expanded section and both directions. Regression: a four-dot chain with its middle section blocked must have no route. This is directly reproduced with a small graph; it is not a claim that every ordinary default-policy game previously encountered this shape.

4. **Fixed and tested — catches and fees were processed in the wrong travel order.** [game.py](railbaron/game.py:318), `_move`. Fees depend on ownership when the pawn uses the section. Previously, the engine priced the entire move, then caught pawns in seat order. A caught pawn already standing at its alternate can immediately collect a payoff and buy track; ownership and the all-sold rate can consequently change before the moving pawn's next step. Movement now charges each section and resolves catches in dot order, reading the current ownership, rate and establishment state each time. Regression: catching a player at Albany who buys NYC results in a bank fee for earlier travel and a $1,000 established fee to the new owner for subsequent travel, rather than just the bank fee. The immediate out-of-turn payoff is retained from the existing engine; its rulebook interpretation is listed below.

5. **Fixed and tested — a resale from the bank could retain the wrong established fee.** [game.py](railbaron/game.py:400), `_transfer`. Example A grants the previous $1,000 bank fee when a line is bought while a pawn is on an incident dot. If that pawn retained a $5,000 concession from an earlier all-sold increase, a bank resale used `setdefault`, leaving $5,000 instead of the current previous $1,000. Acquisition from the bank now replaces that stale rate. Regression: sell back, repurchase, and ride the same line while standing on it.

7. **Fixed and tested — lost twin-city rolls changed later batch payoffs.** [destinations.py](railbaron/destinations.py:238), `transition_matrix`, and `sample_trips` at line 279. The rulebook treats each twin pair as one city; rolling the city occupied loses the turn. The engine correctly retained the original city label, but batch sampling changed Minneapolis into St. Paul after a lost roll. Because three chart entries differ between those twins, the next payoff could change by up to $4,500. Batch sampling now retains the actual starting city; the state-transition matrix puts twin lost-roll probability on the current city. Raw destination odds and the reported rolled city remain separate from the resulting state. Regressions cover both paths.

8. **Fixed and tested — bankruptcy recordings claimed money that was never paid.** [game.py](railbaron/game.py:493), `_settle`; [map_template.html](railbaron/map_template.html:674), `applyEvent` and `buildStates`. The requested deliberate bankruptcy rule pays remaining cash, opponents first in seat order. Previously the `fees` event listed full obligations, so replay transferred too much and made the bankrupt player's balance negative. End-of-turn overwrites hid the error. Events now list actual payments, with original obligations retained separately under `owed` when insolvent. The page checks reconstructed cash rather than replacing it, and the Python recording checker likewise no longer accepts a balance overwrite. A $7,000 debtor owing two opponents $5,000 each now records $5,000 and $2,000, not $10,000. Eight complete 3–6-player browser-event replays were checked in Node, including bankruptcies, against every end-of-turn cash vector.

9. **Fixed and tested — replay canceled a valid declaration when home was also the alternate.** [map_template.html](railbaron/map_template.html:674), `applyEvent`. Such an arrival must pay off, while the declaration remains active until the win is checked after fees. The page cleared `declared` on every `arrive`, although the engine retained it. It now clears declarations only through the appropriate declaration-ending events. Regression exercises that event independently of cash overwrites.

10. **Fixed and tested — analysis helpers returned moves that trapped the pawn.** [board.py](railbaron/board.py:367), `legal_moves`, `reachable`, and `_can_finish` at line 494. The rulebook forbids moving so that the destination cannot be reached without repeating a section. Previously a move using the full roll was reported legal even if its endpoint was a dead end with no unused exit. When a destination is supplied, the helpers now require an unused continuation to it. Memoization also retains distant used track when it can affect that continuation. Regression: at a fork, a one-dot move into a dead end is excluded when the destination is on the other arm. This affects analysis/custom policies; the default engine's complete routes already generally avoided this trap.

11. **Fixed and tested — two displayed/analytical averages had inconsistent denominators.** [map_template.html](railbaron/map_template.html:525), `regionValue`, and [destinations.py](railbaron/destinations.py:250), `long_run`. The page's average excluded lost-turn outcomes, while Python's region recommendation included their zero payoff. Both now show expected payoff per roll. Separately, `long_run` claimed average payoff per completed trip but counted lost destination rolls as zero-payoff trips. It now conditions transitions on leaving the city, while `sample_trips` explicitly documents that its columns are destination rolls, including lost turns. A two-city example where every completed trip pays $10,000 previously reported $3,750; it now reports $10,000. The batch comparison test now compares completed trips with completed trips.

12. **Fixed and tested — an invalid purchase result could partially corrupt ownership.** [game.py](railbaron/game.py:355), `_arrive`. A policy returning `BANK` (`-1`) as its purchase selected the last railroad via Python indexing, deducted its price, changed its owner, then failed on a negative bit shift. The rules allow only a bank-owned railroad or one of the two train purchases. Railroad indices are now validated before those mutations. Regression checks the invalid sentinel is rejected as a railroad purchase.

13. **Suspected but not confirmed — source map encoding and unused payout numbering.** [parse_rbp.py](Map%20Data/parse_rbp.py:43) normalizes route-token dot numbers modulo 1000; [rail_baron_graph.json](Map%20Data/rail_baron_graph.json:3) itself says the special encodings are pending validation. There are 190 encoded tokens with prefixes 1–5. The source format and rulebook intersection diagram are unavailable, so I cannot establish whether stripping those prefixes loses movement topology or only drawing information. Separately, `cities.csv`'s `payout_number` uses a region-grouped numbering that does not index alphabetical `Payout.csv` (e.g. New York's 5 points at Birmingham in this CSV). The engine loads payoffs by normalized names, so this is not a current wrong-payoff lookup. Do not blindly renumber: these may be physical-chart reference numbers. Check both against the original source before relying on them in new analysis.

- All 65 mapped cities have the region of their destination chart. Combined twin-city probabilities match table dice frequencies to the stored rounded precision. Region probability constants match the region chart. Conditional probabilities total exactly 100% in every region; several final digits are adjusted to achieve that total (e.g. 5.557% rather than the exact 5.555…%). These are rounding approximations, not substantive destination errors.
- The payoff chart has **67 data rows and columns**, matching row/column labels and order, symmetric values, zero diagonal, and zero payoff between twins. The two SF/Oakland rows are identical. Minneapolis/St. Paul differ for Albany ($12,500/$12,000), Shreveport ($6,000/$10,500), and Tucumcari ($11,500/$11,000). Only the large Shreveport discrepancy is a strong transcription candidate; retain the other differences pending chart inspection.
- Twins share map places for movement, duplicate-home rejection, lost-turn detection, and home/alternate comparisons. Named payoff lookup preserves the separate 67 chart entries. All **216 dice-face combinations** produce the same outcomes in `Tables`, `Destinations`, and JavaScript; the page consumes the same payload tables.
- All **28 individual railroad graphs are connected**. The whole loaded map is connected. All 748 railroad sections reference valid nodes and codes; there are no duplicated same-railroad sections. Shared sections retain each recorded railroad as separate multigraph edges. The 542 source nodes, 748 edges, and 65 cities agree field-for-field between CSV and JSON. Agreement with an export of the same source is not independent proof of board accuracy.
- The longest drawn adjacent section is about 26.25 source-coordinate units (SC_68–SW_3, AT+SF); the next two are about 25.02 and 24.19. There are no conspicuous continent-spanning endpoint mistakes. Neighboring-city distances and railroad city-service lists were inspected, but physical-board dot counts and omitted shared tracks cannot be certified from internal consistency.
- **PL_2 is probably a twin-city drawing marker, not an omitted movement dot.** [nodes.csv](Map%20Data/nodes.csv:269) stores it at (370,31), only √5 coordinate units from the Minneapolis/St. Paul dot PL_1 at (368,30). - Existing rule cases plus the independent regressions cover normal/bonus shared fees, Express/Superchief eligibility determined before arrival purchases, bonus starts of new trips, establishment examples, permanent all-sold rates after bank resales, alternate payoffs and declaration cancellation, auctions starting left of the seller, the half-price minimum, $500 increments, and bank purchase when nobody bids.
- README commands and module descriptions were checked. Rules stay in `game.py`, decisions in `policy.py`; board/destination helpers remain analytical. The board module's introductory description and batch/average docstrings were corrected. The performance assertions already in the original suite still pass; no speed work was undertaken.

Validation and limits

- **94 tests passed** on Linux Python 3.12.14 with Node 24.19.0 for the JavaScript regressions. The original suite contained 72 tests; 22 independent audit tests were added, and existing replay/batch assertions were strengthened.
- Against an archived copy of the original HEAD, the 22 audit tests gave **16 failures and 6 passes**, establishing the baseline failures. The invalid-purchase test was also separately observed failing before its fix. Two baseline failures are missing enforcement APIs; they establish the added guard rather than an additional default-policy-game outcome.
- **40 full-game comparisons** across 3, 4, 5 and 6 players and ten seeds each produced identical results recorded/unrecorded. Serial and two-worker batches of **60 games each** matched all non-timing summary fields for every player count.
- Rebuilt the tracked generated page with `python -m railbaron.game --no-open --seed 1` using the prepared virtual environment. The new replay cash assertion is included in it.
- No commits, pushes, dependency changes, or `Map Data/` edits. Changes remain in the working tree on `codex_v1`.

Interpretations for Jake to rule on

- **Two declared pawns:** the rule protects the moving declared pawn from being caught. It does not exempt a stationary declared opponent from being caught by that mover. I preserved automatic collection in that case, while the default policy still declines deliberate rover detours when declared. The independent regression confirms the mover remains declared and the stationary pawn pays. Confirm if Jake intends a broader mutual immunity.
- **At-home declaration during a bonus:** “wins immediately” overlaps with the requirement to have the winning cash after turn fees. I followed the requested after-fees interpretation and deferred this win when fees are outstanding. If the declaration fails after fees, the player stays between trips rather than taking that bonus movement; confirm that resolution or specify a different bonus/declaration sequence.
- **Caught while already at the alternate:** the rules say cancellation sends the player to the alternate and payoff occurs once they arrive, but do not explicitly describe already standing there on another player's turn. I retained immediate payoff/purchase and fixed the resulting movement-order charges. If Jake wants to wait until that player's turn instead, that is a distinct rule decision.
- **Meaning of “absolutely necessary” reuse:** implemented as the smallest total number of homeward sections reused, with distance breaking ties. Confirm whether the intended interpretation is instead local necessity at each step. Fresh unused routes always take precedence.
- **Establishment at junctions:** the literal “on a dot that that rail line runs into” grants establishment on every incident line when its rate rises, not solely the line ridden into the dot. The engine follows that wording; the worked examples alone do not narrow it.
- **Twin payoff differences:** the pair is one movement city, but the supplied physical-chart transcription has separate labels and nonidentical Minneapolis/St. Paul rows. I kept the last genuinely reached/rolled label for payoff selection; a lost roll does not change it. Confirm the physical chart before deciding whether differences should be collapsed.
- **Analysis fee helpers:** `Board.turn_fee` and `FeeModel` represent nominal ownership fees and do not carry an established-player state. Thirty ownership scenarios spanning normal and bonus movement matched the engine for ordinary non-established turns; they cannot alone reproduce an established concession. This is an API limitation, not a separately demonstrated game-engine defect. Policies estimating fees also intentionally remain heuristics.

The user-listed simplifications were treated as deliberate: optional rules, seat 1 moving first, bankruptcy payout/elimination and last-player victory, a same-city alternate losing the turn and canceling declaration, skipping the bonus after declared home arrival, fixed $500 auction increments, and omitted courtesy rules. No weak default policy choice was classified as a bug.

Areas not independently verified: the physical map, railroad endpoint/dot-count accuracy, missing shared sections and non-dot intersections, special RBP encoding semantics, the original printed payoff chart, full browser rendering/interaction, and Windows/Python 3.13. This audit exercises many seeded games and adversarial cases; it does not claim exhaustive coverage of every reachable game state.
